Principia Biscotica

On the Variational Optimization of Anthropogenic Cookie Flux in a Discrete-Tick Universe

RYgamer1

zero.minr.org

Abstract. We consider the cookie-clicker minigame of Harder Hat secret 16 (zero.minr.org): a confectionery production system driven by a 20 Hz anthropogenic click field, subject to a millisecond cooldown gate evaluated on a 50 ms tick lattice, with stochastic auric emission into a 14-site boundary lattice and a periodically available lactic amplification field. We formulate the upgrade-acquisition problem as optimal control over a seven-dimensional state manifold of cardinality \(3.78\times10^{8}\) (a space strictly larger than the production goal it serves), derive the steady-state flux by Karush–Kuhn–Tucker analysis of the click-allocation program, solve the milk-hoarding problem as a free-boundary optimal-stopping problem, and characterize the optimal purchase sequence via a payback index policy on the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation. The ground-state completion time is computed to be \(T^{\star}=1365\ \mathrm{s}=22{:}45\), attained by a trajectory of 331 purchases. All constants are exact and taken from the map’s script source (Cookie.nms).

1.The State Manifold

Let the player’s upgrade configuration be the septuple

\begin{equation} \sigma \;=\; (c,\mu,\gamma,\delta,\chi,d,k)\;\in\;\mathcal{S}\;:=\;\mathbb{Z}_{12}\times\mathbb{Z}_{501}\times\mathbb{Z}_{17}\times\mathbb{Z}_{14}\times\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{11}\times\mathbb{Z}_{12}, \end{equation}

recording, respectively, purchases in the cooldown, multiplier, auric-emission, diamond-fraction, milk-unlock, lactic-duration, and lactic-amplitude ladders. We note with some alarm that

\begin{equation} |\mathcal{S}| \;=\; 12\cdot 501\cdot 17\cdot 14\cdot 2\cdot 11\cdot 12 \;=\; 377{,}745{,}984 \;>\; \mathcal{G} := 10^{8}, \end{equation}

i.e. the game admits more distinct upgrade configurations than the number of cookies it demands.1 Each coordinate induces a ladder map into physical parameters, expressible in Iverson-bracket normal form; for instance the cooldown map \(C:\mathbb{Z}_{12}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}\) (milliseconds) is

\begin{equation} C(c) \;=\; 1000\,[c=0] \;+\; \sum_{j=1}^{11} C_j\,[c=j],\qquad (C_j) = (400,300,225,175,125,100,80,65,55,51,50), \end{equation}

with analogous maps \(G(\gamma)\) (auric odds \(1/G\)), \(D(\delta)\) (diamond odds \(1/D\)), \(T(d)\) (lactic window, s), and \(K(k)\) (lactic amplitude); their full tabulations are relegated to Appendix A. The multiplier map alone admits a closed form:

\begin{equation} M(\mu) \;=\; \mu+1. \end{equation}

2.The Click Field and Its Gating

The player’s hand is modeled as a Dirac comb of fundamental frequency \(f_{\!*}=20\) Hz,

\begin{equation} \mathcal{K}(t)\;:=\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta\!\left(t-\tfrac{n}{f_{\!*}}\right), \end{equation}

assumed exactly periodic for all \(t\in[0,T^\star]\); ulnar fatigue is beyond the scope of this work.2 A click at epoch \(t_n\) directed at the principal cookie lands if and only if the cooldown gate opens,

\begin{equation} \Theta\!\bigl(\lfloor t_n\rfloor_{\mathbb{T}} - \lfloor t_{\mathrm{last}}\rfloor_{\mathbb{T}} - C(c)\bigr)\;=\;1, \qquad \mathbb{T}:=50\,\mathbb{Z}\ \text{ms}, \end{equation}

where \(\Theta\) is the Heaviside step and \(\lfloor\cdot\rfloor_{\mathbb{T}}\) denotes quantization onto the server tick lattice — a detail whose consequences for the terminal cooldown tiers are examined in Theorem 2.2. Auric clicks bypass the gate entirely but are drawn from the same 20 Hz hand budget; the two channels therefore compete. Writing \(m\) for the landed principal-click rate, \(s\) for the auric harvest rate, and \(p:=1/G\), the steady-state flux is the value of the linear program

\begin{equation} R_0(\sigma)\;=\;\max_{(m,s)\in\mathbb{R}^2_{\ge 0}}\;\bigl\{\,mM+sV \;\big|\; m+s\le f_{\!*},\;\; m\le \tfrac{1000}{C},\;\; (1-p)\,s\le p\,m \,\bigr\}, \end{equation}

where the third constraint is conservation of spawned aurics (one cannot harvest what has not spawned) and \(V\) is the mean auric value derived in §3. Stationarity of the Lagrangian \(\mathcal{L}=mM+sV-\lambda_1(m+s-f_{\!*})-\lambda_2(m-\tfrac{1000}{C})-\lambda_3((1-p)s-pm)\) together with complementary slackness yields the closed form:

Proposition 2.1 (Click allocation). The program above is solved by \begin{equation} m^{\star}=\min\!\left\{\frac{1000}{C},\; f_{\!*}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{G}\Bigr)\right\}, \qquad s^{\star}=\frac{m^{\star}}{G-1}, \end{equation} the two branches of the min corresponding to the cooldown-bound phase (\(\lambda_2>0\)) and the budget-bound phase (\(\lambda_1>0\)) respectively.

Theorem 2.2 (Obsolescence of the terminal cooldown tiers). Along any trajectory that reaches auric odds \(p\ge \tfrac1{10}\) (i.e. \(\gamma\ge 8\)), the tiers \(c=10\) (51 ms) and \(c=11\) (50 ms) satisfy \begin{equation} \frac{\partial R_0}{\partial c}\bigg|_{c\ge 9} = 0 . \end{equation}

Proof. For \(p\ge\tfrac1{10}\), the budget bound gives \(f_{\!*}(1-p)\le 18 < \tfrac{1000}{55}=18.\overline{18}\), so the min in Proposition 2.1 is already achieved by the budget branch at \(c=9\); further reduction of \(C\) tightens an inactive constraint. The 30,000 cookies those two tiers cost buy nothing. ∎

3.Auric Emission and the Boundary Lattice

Every landed click, principal or auric, performs an independent Bernoulli(\(p\)) trial; success emits one auric quantum onto a uniformly chosen site of the boundary lattice \(\mathfrak{S}\), \(|\mathfrak{S}|=14\), the emission being annihilated if the site is occupied. Each quantum carries a species drawn from the measure

\begin{equation} \nu \;=\; \Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{D}\Bigr)\,\delta_{\mathrm{Au}} \;+\; \tfrac{1}{D}\,\delta_{\mathrm{C}}, \end{equation}

where Au denotes the golden species (worth \(20M\)) and C the diamond species3 (worth \(200M\)), so the mean harvested value per quantum is

\begin{equation} V(\sigma) \;=\; M\!\int w\,\mathrm{d}\nu \;=\; M\Bigl[\,20\Bigl(1-\frac1D\Bigr) + \frac{200}{D}\Bigr] \;=\; M\Bigl(20+\frac{180}{D}\Bigr). \end{equation}

Under the eager-harvest policy the lattice is a.s. empty at emission epochs and annihilation is negligible. For completeness we record that a lazy harvester with offered load \(a=s^\star\) faces the Erlang loss probability

\begin{equation} B(a,14) \;=\; \frac{a^{14}/14!}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{14} a^{j}/j!}\,, \end{equation}

the verification of which is left as an exercise.

4.Lactic Field Theory

Once \(\chi=1\) (a 15,000-cookie tithe), the lactic field may be excited once per period \(\Lambda=45\) s, remaining active for \(T(d)\) seconds during which all gains are amplified by \(K(k)\). The economically interesting phenomenon is hoarding: suspending auric harvest for the final \(\tau\) seconds of downtime so that quanta accumulate on \(\mathfrak{S}\) and are harvested under amplification. Occupancy \(b(\tau)\) obeys the saturating kinetics

\begin{equation} \frac{\mathrm{d}b}{\mathrm{d}\tau} \;=\; a\Bigl(1-\frac{b}{14}\Bigr),\qquad b(0)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b(\tau) \;=\; 14\bigl(1-e^{-a\tau/14}\bigr), \end{equation}

since a full lattice annihilates its own emissions. Hoarding trades foregone unamplified harvest \(aV\tau\) against amplified recovery \(K V b(\tau)\); the hoard functional is therefore

\begin{equation} J(\tau) \;=\; K V\, b(\tau) \;-\; aV\tau . \end{equation}

Theorem 4.1 (Optimal hoarding horizon). The functional \(J\) is uniquely maximized on \([0,\Lambda-T]\) at \begin{equation} \tau^{\star} \;=\; \min\!\left\{\Lambda-T,\;\; \frac{14}{a}\,\ln K\right\}. \end{equation}

Proof. \(J'(\tau)=aV\bigl(Ke^{-a\tau/14}-1\bigr)\) vanishes iff \(e^{a\tau/14}=K\), and \(J''(\tau)=-\tfrac{a^{2}KV}{14}e^{-a\tau/14}<0\) everywhere, so the interior critical point is the global maximum; the boundary clamp handles the case where downtime ends first. ∎

Averaging over one period, the milk-aware flux is

\begin{equation} \bar R(\sigma) \;=\; \frac{1}{\Lambda}\Bigl[(\Lambda-T)\,R_0 \;+\; T K R_0 \;+\; \chi\, J(\tau^\star)^{+}\Bigr]. \end{equation}

At the terminal configuration (\(K=20\), \(T=20\), \(a=10\)): \(\tau^\star = 1.4\ln 20 \approx 4.19\) s, \(b(\tau^\star)\approx 13.3\) quanta, and \(J(\tau^\star)\approx 4.88\times 10^{6}\) cookies per period — the effective amplification averages \(\tfrac{25+20\cdot 20}{45}\approx 9.44\times\) before the hoard is even counted.

5.The Purchase Functional and Its Bellman Equation

Between purchases the bank evolves as \(\dot B = \bar R(\sigma)\); a purchase \(u\) at price \(P_u\) induces the jump \((\sigma,B)\mapsto(\sigma\oplus u,\;B-P_u)\). A strategy is a purchase sequence \(\pi=(u_1,\dots,u_N)\), and the object of the entire enterprise is the completion-time functional

\begin{equation} T[\pi] \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bigl(P_{u_i}-B_{i^-}\bigr)^{+}}{\bar R(\sigma_{i-1})}\;+\;\frac{\mathcal{G}-B_{N}}{\bar R(\sigma_N)}, \qquad T^{\star}\;=\;\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\;T[\pi]. \end{equation}

The value function \(W(\sigma,B)\) (time-to-go) satisfies the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman quasi-variational inequality

\begin{equation} \min\!\Biggl\{\,\bar R(\sigma)\,\frac{\partial W}{\partial B}+1,\;\; \min_{u\in\mathcal{U}(\sigma),\,P_u\le B}\bigl[\,W(\sigma\oplus u,\,B-P_u)-W(\sigma,B)\,\bigr]\Biggr\}=0, \qquad W(\cdot,\mathcal{G})=0, \end{equation}

whose exact solution over \(|\mathcal{S}|\times[0,\mathcal{G}]\) is left as an exercise for a larger computer. In practice the optimum is attained (to within measurement) by the payback index policy: at each decision epoch, buy

\begin{equation} u^{\star} \;=\; \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{u\,:\,\Delta\bar R_u>0}\; \underbrace{\frac{(P_u-B)^{+}}{\bar R(\sigma)}}_{\text{saving up}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{P_u}{\Delta \bar R_u}}_{\text{amortization}}, \qquad \Delta\bar R_u:=\bar R(\sigma\oplus u)-\bar R(\sigma), \end{equation}

subject to the admissibility (myopic-improvement) criterion that the purchase strictly reduce the projected finish time,

\begin{equation} \frac{(P_u-B)^{+}}{\bar R(\sigma)}+\frac{\mathcal{G}-B'}{\bar R(\sigma\oplus u)} \;<\; \frac{\mathcal{G}-B}{\bar R(\sigma)}, \end{equation}

terminating (all further purchases inadmissible) in a final sprint to \(\mathcal{G}\).

Remark (a false vacuum). A width-600 beam search over the same lattice relaxed into a metastable configuration at \(T=7585\) s, from which the index policy tunnels directly to \(T^\star=1365\) s.

6.Arithmetic of the Multiplier Ladder

The multiplier stage prices \(P_\mu(i)=10+5i(i+1)\), \(i=0,\dots,499\), assemble into the ordinary generating function

\begin{equation} \sum_{i=0}^{\infty}P_\mu(i)\,x^{i} \;=\; \frac{10}{1-x}\;+\;\frac{10\,x}{(1-x)^{3}}, \end{equation}

from which (equivalently, by Faulhaber’s formula) the cumulative cost of the first \(n\) stages is the cubic

\begin{equation} \Phi(n)\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\bigl(10+5i(i+1)\bigr) \;=\; 10n+\frac{5}{3}\bigl(n^{3}-n\bigr). \end{equation}

Theorem 6.1 (Insufficiency of the ladder’s tail). Completing the multiplier ladder costs \begin{equation} \Phi(500) \;=\; 208{,}337{,}500 \;>\; 2\,\mathcal{G}, \end{equation} and the tail beyond the optimal stopping stage \(\mu^\star=271\) alone costs \(\Phi(500)-\Phi(271)=175{,}164{,}390>\mathcal{G}\). The trajectory that maxes the multiplier before pressing the Secret Completion Button has therefore, by construction, already earned the goal 1.75 times over and spent it on the wrong thing.

7.The Ground State

Assembling §§2–4 into a single object, the stationary flux admits the following fully reduced form, displayed once in full:

\begin{equation} \bar R(\sigma)=\frac{1}{45}\left[ \Bigl(45+\chi\,(K-1)\,T\Bigr) \underbrace{\min\Bigl\{\tfrac{1000}{C},\,20\bigl(1-\tfrac1G\bigr)\Bigr\}}_{m^\star} (\mu{+}1)\Bigl(1+\tfrac{20+\frac{180}{D}}{G-1}\Bigr) +\chi\!\left(14K\,\Xi\Bigl(1-e^{-\frac{m^\star}{14(G-1)}\,\tau^\star}\Bigr)-\tfrac{m^\star \tau^\star}{G-1}\,\Xi\right)^{\!+} \right] \end{equation}

with \(\Xi:=(\mu+1)\bigl(20+\tfrac{180}{D}\bigr)\) and \(\tau^\star\) as in Theorem 4.1. Maximizing \(T[\pi]^{-1}\) over the 377,745,984-point lattice by the index policy of §5 yields the empirical ground state:

\begin{equation} \sigma^{\star}=(9,\,271,\,16,\,13,\,1,\,10,\,11):\qquad C=55\ \mathrm{ms},\;\; M=272,\;\; G=2,\;\; D=3,\;\; T=20\ \mathrm{s},\;\; K=20, \end{equation}
\begin{equation} m^{\star}=s^{\star}=10\ \mathrm{s}^{-1},\qquad V=21{,}760,\qquad R_0=220{,}320\ \mathrm{s}^{-1},\qquad \bar R\approx 2.19\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{s}^{-1}, \end{equation}

at which point \(98.8\%\) of income flows through the auric channel and the principal cookie is reduced to a ceremonial role. Defining the strategy partition function at inverse temperature \(\beta\),

\begin{equation} Z(\beta)\;=\;\sum_{\pi\in\Pi} e^{-\beta\,T[\pi]}, \qquad T^{\star}\;=\;-\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta}\,\ln Z(\beta), \end{equation}

the zero-temperature limit selects the 331-purchase trajectory with

\begin{equation} \boxed{\;T^{\star} \;=\; 1365\ \mathrm{s} \;=\; 22{:}45\;} \end{equation}

during which \(136{,}406{,}095\) cookies are reinvested and roughly \(2.36\times10^{8}\) are earned in total.

8.The Grand Unified Cookie Equation

Substituting §§1–6 into §7 gives the entire model — hand, gate, aurics, milk, hoard, wallet, and the minimization itself — as a single expression. The symbol \(m_i^{\star}\) is defined by its own underbrace and reused in the third line.

\begin{equation} \begin{split} T^{\star}\;=\; &\;\underbrace{-\,\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\;\frac{1}{\beta}\,\ln\!\!\sum_{\pi\in\Pi}\,\exp}_{\text{minimization over all purchase orders}} \Biggl(\,-\beta\sum_{i=0}^{|\pi|}\; \underbrace{45\,\Bigl[\,\bigl(P_{u_{i+1}}-B_i\bigr)^{+}\,\bigl[\,i\lt|\pi|\,\bigr]\;+\;\bigl(10^{8}-B_i\bigr)\,\bigl[\,i=|\pi|\,\bigr]\Bigr]}_{\text{the wallet, per lactic period}} \;\times\\[6pt] &\Biggl\{\; \underbrace{\Bigl(45+\chi_i\bigl(K(k_i)-1\bigr)\,T(d_i)\Bigr)}_{\text{lactic amplification}}\, \underbrace{\min\Biggl\{1000\Bigl(\textstyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{11}C_j\,[\,c_i=j\,]\Bigr)^{\!-1}\!,\;\;20\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{G(\gamma_i)}\Bigr)\Biggr\}}_{=:\;m_i^{\star}\ \ \text{(the hand meets the gate)}} \;\underbrace{\bigl(\mu_i+1\bigr)}_{\text{the multiplier}}\; \underbrace{\Biggl(1+\Bigl(20+\frac{180}{D(\delta_i)}\Bigr)\bigl(G(\gamma_i)-1\bigr)^{-1}\Biggr)}_{\text{the auric channel}}\\[6pt] &\;+\;\chi_i\,\bigl(\mu_i+1\bigr)\Bigl(20+\frac{180}{D(\delta_i)}\Bigr)\Biggl(\, \underbrace{14\,K(k_i)\biggl(1-\exp\Bigl(-\frac{1}{14}\min\Bigl\{\frac{\bigl(45-T(d_i)\bigr)\,m_i^{\star}}{G(\gamma_i)-1},\;14\ln K(k_i)\Bigr\}\Bigr)\biggr)}_{\text{the hoard}} \;-\; \underbrace{\min\Bigl\{\frac{\bigl(45-T(d_i)\bigr)\,m_i^{\star}}{G(\gamma_i)-1},\;14\ln K(k_i)\Bigr\}}_{\text{forgone unamplified harvest}} \Biggr)^{\!\!+}\;\Biggr\}^{-1}\,\Biggr)\\[8pt] &\;\text{subject to}\quad B_0=0,\qquad \sigma_0=\mathbf{0},\qquad B_{i+1}=\bigl(B_i-P_{u_{i+1}}\bigr)^{+},\qquad \sigma_{i+1}=\sigma_i\oplus u_{i+1},\\[6pt] &\;=\;1365\ \mathrm{s}\;=\;22{:}45. \end{split} \end{equation}

Every quantity above is exact: the Iverson sum in the denominator of \(m_i^\star\) is the cooldown ladder of eq. (3); the factor \(\bigl(20+\tfrac{180}{D}\bigr)\) is the species integral of eq. (11); the nested min inside the exponential is Theorem 4.1 with \(a\tau^\star\) pre-multiplied; the outer \((\cdot)^{+}\) is the hoard admissibility of eq. (16); the bank recursion encodes the save-up waits of eq. (17); and the zero-temperature limit performs the minimization of eq. (27) over all \(|\Pi|\) purchase orderings so that the reader does not have to. Evaluation of the right-hand side is by inspection.4
